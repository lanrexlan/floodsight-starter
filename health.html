FloodSight Health Intelligence — Lagos Pilot

Floods predict malaria outbreaks 17–24 days early

When Lagos floods, stagnant water creates the breeding conditions for Anopheles gambiae. FloodSight Health tracks inundation in real time and alerts Community Health Extension Workers before the first case spike — so nets and rapid tests are pre-positioned, not reactive.

The pipeline

From satellite rain to CHEW alert in hours

A logistic model converts flood inundation area, peak susceptibility, and ambient temperature into an outbreak probability. When a pilot LGA crosses the threshold, CHEWs receive an actionable SMS before the larval development window closes.

Quasi-experimental MEL design

Grand Challenges Canada requires rigorous evidence. FloodSight Health uses a difference-in-differences design across 10 Lagos LGAs to isolate the impact of early CHEW alerts on malaria case incidence at Month 18.

Scientific basis

Built on peer-reviewed entomology

Every parameter in the outbreak model traces to a published source. The breeding lag table, logistic coefficients, and outbreak window are grounded in field studies from Lagos and comparable West African settings.

📐
Temperature-adjusted development
At Lagos dry-season temperatures (26–32 °C), Anopheles gambiae larval development takes 8–14 days. FloodSight uses a lookup table calibrated to local conditions so the alert window tightens in heat and widens when it cools.
Bayoh & Lindsay (2003) · Med. Vet. Entomol.
📍
Stagnant water as primary breeding site
Floodwater that recedes into shallow pools with low turbulence and organic matter is the dominant larval habitat for urban Anopheles. Inundation area is our primary predictor; the model fires only above 0.5 km² per LGA grid.
Edeghere et al. (2018) · Malar. J.
⏱️
17–24 day flood-to-case lag
Epidemiological studies in Lagos State show confirmed malaria case spikes 17–24 days after major flooding events — consistent with the combined larval development plus extrinsic incubation period. This is the window FloodSight targets.
Oduola et al. (2012) · Acta Trop.
📡
CHEW-based early action
Nigeria's Community Health Extension Workers reach the last mile without smartphones. SMS-based alert-and-respond systems have shown 60–80 % action rates in comparable West African CHEW programs, enabling pre-positioning rather than reactive treatment.
WHO AFRO (2022) · Community Health Evidence Review
