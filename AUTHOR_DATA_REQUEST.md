# FloodSight request for Lagos research data

Status updated 2026-10-05: the owner reports that the request was sent and supplied
the author's reply. **Underlying-data sharing declined; no files or commercial
reuse permission acquired.** The reasons given were institutional/ethical
restrictions on commercial sharing, third-party redistribution agreements, and
ongoing confidential research/publication commitments. The author pointed to
public datasets and methods in the paper instead. This status is based on the
owner-provided correspondence, not independent access to an email account.

The owner subsequently supplied the author's follow-up listing public providers
and five empirical studies. The list is recorded and assessed in
[AUTHOR_SOURCE_PLAN.md](AUTHOR_SOURCE_PLAN.md). It grants no additional rights to
restricted study files. A closing acknowledgement was drafted in chat; sending
that acknowledgement has not been verified here. No further request to this
author is needed.

Decision: close this request as unavailable for the present validation intake.
Do not seek restricted files through another coauthor as a workaround. Retain the
paper as a methodological reference and its selected numerical summaries as
research-only QC, not measured depth truth or FloodSight accuracy evidence.
Acquire public source data directly under their own terms; prioritize independent
local measurements and prospective wet/dry observation. No reply was sent here.

To: Opeyemi Aniramu <samuelaniramu@gmail.com>

Subject: Request for Lagos flood observations and model data for FloodSight validation

Dear Opeyemi Aniramu,

I am developing FloodSight, a flood-risk and early-warning platform for Lagos. I
read your Scientific Reports paper, “Remote sensing and GIS-based modelling of
land use dynamics and urban flood risk in Lagos megacity for future flood
mitigation” (DOI: 10.1038/s41598-026-38544-1), and would like to request the
underlying data referenced in its data-availability statement.

Our immediate priority is independent validation. We are keeping simulated
results separate from observed flood depths and are not treating published model
performance as evidence of FloodSight's accuracy.

If available and shareable, could you provide:

- Flood-impact locations and any field-measured depths, with observation dates,
  coordinates/site IDs, ground reference, measurement methods and uncertainty;
  confirmed dry observations would also be valuable.
- Rainfall and streamflow records used for calibration and validation, including
  station metadata, units, timestamps, quality flags, and the calibration/test
  periods.
- Georeferenced flood-extent/depth outputs, basin boundaries, HEC-HMS project
  files, and the principal parameters and forcing data behind Tables 6-8.

If field-measured depths were not collected, confirmation of that distinction
would help us identify the appropriate use of the results. Anonymised site IDs
would be sufficient; we do not need residents' names or other personal details.

FloodSight is intended for eventual commercial deployment. Could you confirm
which uses you can authorise, including internal benchmarking, model development
and validation, and publication of derived findings? We would not redistribute
source files or adapted maps without appropriate permission. If third-party
agency data require separate approval, please let us know whom to contact.

We would cite your paper and dataset, follow any attribution or access conditions,
and would be happy to discuss a data-sharing agreement or research collaboration.
Even a limited validation subset would be helpful if the full archive cannot be
shared.

Thank you for considering this request.
